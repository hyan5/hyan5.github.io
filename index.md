## About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the [Department of Computer Science](https://cs.gmu.edu) and [GMU NLP Group](https://nlp.cs.gmu.edu/) at George Mason University, advised by [Dr. Ziyu Yao](https://ziyuyao.org/), where I work on making **Large Language Models safe, trustworthy, and usable for everyone**. My research covers four connected areas.

1. **🛡️ LLM Safety & Secure Code Generation**. I study when and why LLMs write vulnerable code. My work explores how models can be guided to find and fix their own security flaws.
2. **🔬 LLM Interpretability & Explainability**. I look inside LLMs to understand how they actually work. My recent work interprets how LLMs generate code and uses these insights to guide them toward safer and more reliable code.
3. **🤖 LLM Reasoning & Agentic AI**. I study how well LLMs reason and follow instructions. My work examines whether models can faithfully simulate personas with different skill levels in math reasoning. Beyond reasoning, I have a strong interest in Agentic AI, especially in how multiple agents collaborate and how agents manage their memory over long-horizon tasks.
4. **🤝 Human-AI Interaction & Conversational Code Generation**. I design systems that let people create software by talking to LLMs in plain language. I built a conversational assistant that helps non-programmers write correct code, and I taught LLMs to simulate real user feedback so that interactive systems can be trained at scale.

In the summers of 2025 and 2026, I worked at **Amazon** in Seattle as an **Applied Scientist Intern**, where I gained hands-on experience applying LLM research to real-world products at industry scale. Prior to my Ph.D., I received my M.S. in Computer Science from George Mason University (2020) and my B.E. in Electronic and Information Engineering from Fujian Normal University (2017).

Outside of research, I enjoy basketball, badminton, and hiking. I am also a foodie who loves exploring new restaurants and cooking at home.
